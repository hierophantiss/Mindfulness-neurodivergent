FAQ – Neurodivergent Mindfulness App Questions & Answers

What is grounding? — Grounding is the sensation of your body in space through gravity. You feel your soles on the floor, the weight on the chair, the axis of your spine. Gravity is the proof of "Here" — it shows you exactly where you exist in this moment.

Why do we start with the body, not thought? — Inside the body there are no concepts — only sensations. Weight, warmth, pressure, pulse. When you turn to the body, you leave thinking without fighting thinking. This shift — from words to sensations — is the foundation of every mindfulness practice.

How does grounding help against self-criticism? — The Default Mode Network — the "autopilot" — is associated with mental wandering, self-criticism, and replaying old stories. Every time you feel gravity and your feet on the floor, you restore your physical sensation and activate your proprioceptive center. Although the DMN doesn't permanently stop with just gravity—that requires hyperfocus and open awareness deeper down—grounding acts as the crucial first anchor that reconnects us to the Now, breaking the vicious cycle of thought.

I don't feel anything when I try to ground. What do I do? — This is very common — especially for neurodivergent people. Many have atypical interoception, meaning difficulty perceiving internal body sensations. Try external anchors instead: hold something heavy, press your feet firmly into the floor, feel the temperature of an object. The sensation doesn't need to be strong — even a faint awareness is enough.

What is the "echo" in the body? — The tightness in your body, the tension, the voice that says "you're not doing enough" — they didn't originate inside you. They are echoes of the past: words you heard, looks you received, expectations that weren't met. Your nervous system stored them. This method doesn't ask you to fight that echo — it asks you to recognize it: "Ah, that's the old voice. I don't need to follow it."

What role does proprioception play? — Proprioception is the sense of your body's position in space. When you feel gravity, you activate the proprioceptive system — Craig (2002) Nature Reviews Neuroscience. This sensation competes with the DMN (the autopilot) and brings the mind to "here" without effort.

How are body and space connected? — Deep relaxation in the body is never an isolated act. It always requires awareness of space. Our body, through sensory organs, continuously informs us of our position in space. When space is perceived as safe, the nervous system relaxes — and only then does the body truly let go.

Why is breathing important? — If the body unites us with the earth, breath connects us with the rhythm of life. Every inhale: beginning. Every pause: now. Every exhale: acceptance. Body + Breath together form the vertical axis: gravity gives us "Here," breath gives us "Now."

What is "internal touch"? — Breath is not just air — it is touch. Feel the air at your nostrils, the expansion of the chest, the soft movement of the belly. This internal touch activates interoception — the bridge between body and consciousness.

Which breathing pattern should I use? — It depends on what you need: • 4-2-6-1 (Fourfold Axis): Grounding & Presence. Slow exhale activates the vagus nerve. • 4-7-8 (Dr. Weil): Deep calm & sleep. Long hold balances. • 5-5 (Coherence): Focus & balance. Cardiac coherence — McCraty et al.

Why does slow exhale calm us? — Slow exhale activates the vagus nerve — the "brake" of the nervous system. This reduces heart rate and sends a safety signal to the brain — Gerritsen & Band (2018). A 6-second exhale is enough for activation.

My breathing is shallow/tight. What does it mean? — Breathing is the indicator of your mental state. If tight or shallow → tension. If slow and deep → relaxation. Don't try to change it immediately — first observe. Observation without intervention is the practice itself.

What do I do in a moment of intense panic? — In moments of intense anxiety, let the exhale flow out through the mouth, a little slower and more prolonged. If panic is very intense, use SOS mode — theta binaural beats (6 Hz) + 4-7-8 breath + calming light. Tap the ∞ and select "Overwhelm."

What are the three modes of attention? — • Focused (Closed): Like a flashlight — illuminates one point intensely. • Open: Like ambient light — illuminates many things at once, without focus. • Scattered: Like a strobe — jumps from thought to thought without returning. The goal isn't to eliminate scattering — but to recognize which mode you're in.

What is hyperfocus and how do I break it? — The opposite extreme: the mind locks onto one point and cannot leave. The world around disappears — space, body, time. Tool: Space (soften your gaze, feel the space around) + Body (feel gravity — it "breaks" the tunnel).

What is "labeling" and how does it help? — When a thought pulls you, label it: "Worry," "Scenario," "Criticism." The label creates distance — you are not the thought, you are the one observing it. Then return gently to the axis. You don't need to analyze the thought — just characterize it.

My attention leaves immediately. Am I failing? — Returning attention is not failure — it is the practice itself. Every time you notice attention has left and you return, you're doing a "push-up" for the prefrontal cortex. The number of returns matters — not the duration of focus.

What does "Fourfold Attention" mean? — In the full practice, attention does not work alone. It focuses upon the vertical axis (Body + Breath), and from there opens into Space. Four centers, one presence. This is what "Fourfold Axis" means.

What does "open awareness" mean? — Open awareness is the ability to receive everything without clinging to anything. Thoughts, sensations, sounds — they come and go like clouds in an open sky. You are not the clouds — you are the space that holds them.

How does space help with overload? — Open attention functions as a safety signal to the brain: "no danger, there is space." Peripheral vision, open hearing, the sense of space around — all together they "turn off" the alarm signal. Instead of fighting stimuli, you let them be.

How do I soften my gaze? — Three steps: 1. Focus on one point (Attention). 2. Soften your gaze — don't hold, let the eyes relax. 3. Let space embrace you — sounds, body, air, all together. You're not trying to see everything. You're letting everything exist.

What's the relationship between space and the sky metaphor? — The sky is not burdened by clouds, does not react, does not judge. It simply holds them. The space of awareness works exactly like this — thoughts, emotions, sensations appear and disappear. You remain. This is not a poetic image — it is the experience of the 4th axis.

My mind is "racing." What do I do? — Racing mind = Scattered Attention / Overload. The world becomes a chain of small hooks — each stimulus holds a piece of attention. Tool: Body (Grounding) + Breath (Rhythm). Gravity brings you "here," rhythm brings you "now." Feel your soles + one breath. That's enough.

My mind is "locking." What do I do? — Locking mind = Anchored Attention / Hyperfocus. Like a tunnel of intense focus. Time disappears, the body is forgotten. Tool: Space (Release) + Body (Return). Soften your gaze, feel the space around — this "breaks" the tunnel.

What is the "mechanical mind"? — Many neurodivergent people develop a "mechanical mind" — an internal rule system that replaces the missing automaticity. It's exhausting but brilliantly clever. Mindfulness doesn't replace the mechanical mind — it relieves it. It gives moments of rest to the system.

Why is self-criticism so powerful? — Research suggests that harsh self-criticism can engage the same threat-response circuitry that reacts to external danger, involving the amygdala and stress hormones like cortisol. Every time you judge yourself, your brain reacts as if under attack. Self-criticism isn't your truth — it's an echo. The gentleness of this method is the very attitude that's needed.

My mind isn't broken? — No. The neurodivergent mind is not defective — it works differently. The intensity, the scattering, the locking are not weaknesses — they are patterns. And patterns can be recognized. You don't fight your brain — you collaborate with it.

How do I use the Fourfold Axis at work? — 3 breaths before opening email. Feel your soles before speaking in a meeting. Label anxious thoughts. These 3 things, every day, change your relationship with tension without needing extra time.

What do I do in a noisy space? — • Feel your feet on the ground — instant grounding. • 3 quiet breaths with mouth exhale. • Open peripheral vision — it transforms stimuli into "clouds." • Headphones or fidget toy if needed — it's not weakness, it's regulation.

How does it help in social situations? — • Find a neutral focus point (plant, wall). • Feel your feet steady — grounding during interaction. • Tension in jaw? One slow exhale releases it. • If you need to, step out for 30 seconds — no justification needed.

How much time does it need daily? — Consistency in invisible doses matters more than duration. 5 seconds of grounding before starting the day. 10 seconds of breathing before sleep. That's enough. Presence doesn't need hours — it starts with victories of a few seconds.

What do I do in an anxiety crisis? — Anxiety Protocol — 4 steps: 1. Weight: feel gravity 2. Exhale: 3 slow mouth exhales 3. Label: "Worry" or "Scenario" 4. Space: open peripheral vision You can do just one step. Even that is enough.

What do I do in sensory overload? — Overload Protocol — 4 steps: 1. Soles: feel them on the floor 2. Flow: stay with the breath flow 3. One point: stable focus point 4. Soften: stimuli are clouds If too intense, use SOS mode.

What are the early warning signs? — The first signals appear in the body before thought: jaw tightening, shoulder tension, chest pressure, shallow breathing. Recognize your own — these are your early indicator. The sooner you catch them, the easier they are to manage.

How do I use the 4 steps? — Do the steps in order or choose the one you need now: BODY: Feel your weight. BREATH: One mouth exhale. ATTENTION: Where is your attention? Return to a point. SPACE: Soften. Remember the sky. Even one step changes the state.

Do I have to do them in order? — No. You can start from any step. Feeling tension → BODY first. Mind racing → BREATH. Attention jumping → ATTENTION. Feeling closed → SPACE. The S→B→A→S sequence is the full practice, but one step alone is enough.

How do I do the full practice? — Stage 1 — Grounding: Sit. Feel contact points. Stage 2 — Breath: Close eyes. Follow the flow. Stage 3 — Attention: Open eyes. Stable point. Label if needed. Stage 4 — Space: Soften. Feel everything together. Even 20 seconds is enough.

What attitude should I have? — Gentleness is not optional — it is the method itself. Every time you return without criticism, every time you accept distraction as part of the process — you are practicing kindness. Treat yourself the way you would treat a friend who is struggling.

How long does each stage last? — There is no right duration. Start with 5 seconds per stage (20 seconds total). If you want, increase to 1 minute per stage (4 minutes total). Consistency matters — not duration. 20 seconds daily > 20 minutes once.

What does neuroscience say about this method? — • Gravity sense activates the proprioceptive system — Craig (2002). • Slow exhale activates the vagus nerve — Gerritsen & Band (2018). • Mindfulness reduces DMN activity — Brewer et al. (2011) PNAS. • 8 weeks of practice measurably change brain structure — Hölzel et al. (2011). • Focused attention engages the prefrontal cortex, central to self-regulation.

Which traditions does the method come from? — • Satipatthana Sutta (Theravada): The Four Foundations correspond to the four axes. • Samatha & Vipassana: Focus → insight, the classic path 3rd → 4th axis. • Dzogchen (Nyingma): Rigpa — the natural state of open awareness = 4th axis. • Sufism (Inayatiyya): Breath as bridge between matter and spirit. • Tai Chi / Qi Gong: Grounding through movement — gravity as teacher.

Does the brain actually change? — Yes, measurably. Hölzel et al. (2011) showed that 8 weeks of mindfulness practice increases grey matter in areas related to learning, memory, emotional regulation, and decision-making. This is called neuroplasticity — the brain changes structure based on what you do repeatedly. Even 5 seconds of observation strengthens the insula.
