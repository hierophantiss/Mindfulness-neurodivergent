Chapter 2: Breath / Air – The Energy of the Present Self

The breath is the element of Air, connecting us with the continuous rhythm of life and teaching the acceptance of transience.

Breath (Air) is the rhythm of 'Now'. Exhalation is the relaxation switch. Do not control it, just feel the touch of the air.

If the body unites us with the earth, the breath connects us with the sky and the ceaseless flow of the world.

Each inhalation: a beginning. Each pause: now. Each exhalation: acceptance.

The breath is not just air — it is touch. Feel the air in the nostrils, the expansion of the chest, the soft movement of the belly. This inner touch activates interoception — the bridge between body and consciousness.

Body + Breath together form the vertical axis: gravity gives us the 'Here' (vertical stability), the breath the 'Now' (horizontal flow). This cross — Stability x Rhythm — is the foundation upon which attention is built.

If it is tight or shallow → tension. If it is slow and deep → relaxation. The breath is the indicator of our mental state.

Emotions are winds too — weather phenomena in the body and the breath, not ideas in the head. That is where they can be read, before words are found for them.

• Anger: a strong rising wind — it inflates the chest, makes the breath shallow. • Anxiety: a wind that keeps changing direction — breath erratic, without rhythm. • Sadness: stillness and dampness — breath thin, with sighs that come on their own. • Numbness: dense fog — breath so faint it is forgotten. • Calm: a light steady breeze. The map is not all bad weather.

First read the weather: what wind is this, where do you feel it, how long has it been blowing? No "why" — only the weather, now. Then accompany the wind with regulation: at the crest a long exhale, then balanced breathing (5 in, 5 out), or sound on the exhale.

Not all winds obey the same tools. Gusts (a sudden flash of irritation) soften within minutes through the breath. Weather systems (the pain of a rejection) last for days — if the breathing does not extinguish them, you have not failed: here the goal is to keep the vessel sailable while the wind blows.

And there is climate — long patterns that come from far back and resolve over years, often with professional support. But knowing your climate changes everything in between: when the wind rises out of proportion to the trigger, you no longer say "something is wrong with me" — you say "I know this pattern". The pain remains; what leaves is the second pain, the confusion and self-blame. You are not broken. You have weather — like everyone. Now you also have a map.

In moments of intense anxiety, we let the exhale come out through the mouth, slightly slower and more prolonged. The slow exhale activates the vagus nerve — the 'brake' of the nervous system, helping us self-regulate.

Producing sound (like humming) creates a vibration that massages the vagus nerve, calming the body. Sound naturally elongates the exhalation without effort.
