Never Force – The Art of Effortless Practice

The Dzogchen teaching advises us never to force or overstrain our energy, but always, in all circumstances that we meet, to be aware of its limits. If sometimes you don’t feel like sitting down to practice, you shouldn’t force yourself. There may be some unknown problem connected with our energy. In such cases it is important to be able to relax and give ourselves a break, so as not to create obstacles to progress in the practice. Problems of loneliness, depression, mental disorders and so on also often arise from an unbalanced state of our energy.

The mind affects the state of the body and the state of energy. At the same time it depends on them. Sometimes the mind is completely enslaved by energy, and it can only be brought into a state of equilibrium by first removing the disturbances of energy. It is very important to understand the interdependence between mind and energy. In all Buddhist traditions, when a person is taught to meditate, they explain that the breathing should be slow and deep in order to create favourable conditions for the development of a calm state of mind. If we take a nervous person whose mind is in an agitated state, we can immediately see that their breathing is rapid and shallow. Sometimes it is not possible to calm the mind through meditation alone, and in order to restore the balance of energy one has to practise Yantra Yoga and breathing exercises.

As an example to illustrate our relative condition, the comparison with a cage is often used. A person is like a little bird in a cage, which both deprives it of freedom and protects it. The cage here is the symbol of all the limitations of body, speech and mind. But in this example the cage does not mean some terrible, extraordinary situation — it refers only to the ordinary conditions in which a human being lives. The problem is that we do not realise the situation in which we actually find ourselves, and essentially we are afraid to realise it, because we have grown up in this cage from the earliest age.

Let us see how a child falls into these limitations. During the first months of life, when the infant still cannot reason or speak, happy parents cradle it in their arms and whisper affectionate words to it. But when the child begins to walk and wants to touch something, they say: “Don’t touch that! Don’t go there!” As the child grows up, they are forced to restrict more and more their way of expressing themselves (the manner of sitting at the table, eating, and so on), until they become a well-behaved child. Then the parents are proud of them, but the truth is that the poor little one is forced to completely copy their way of thinking. The child is taught to live in the cage. Then, at five or six years old, the child begins to go to school, which requires that they follow certain rules and fulfil their parents’ expectations. The child has to overcome some difficulties, but gradually gets used to this additional cage as well.

In our time it takes many years to build the cage in which we cannot live in society without it. In addition, there are many additional factors of dependence, such as political ideas, religious beliefs, bonds of friendship, work together, and so on. If the cage is strong enough, we are ready to live in it and we feel protected. This is the condition of every human being and we must discover it by observing ourselves.

When we become aware of our limitations, there is the possibility of overcoming them.

The bird living in the cage gives birth to its chicks in the same cage. When they are born, the little birds already have wings. Although they cannot fly in the cage, the fact that they are born with wings shows that their true nature is to fly in the open sky. But if a bird that has always lived in the cage leaves it, it may encounter many dangers, because it does not know what awaits it outside the cage. It may be eaten by a hawk or caught by a cat. Therefore the bird needs to train gradually, flying gently in a limited space until, feeling ready, it can set off in flight with confidence.

The same applies to us: even if it is difficult for us to overcome all limitations immediately, it is important to know that our true state, which is independent of everything, is always with us and that we really always have the possibility of discovering it.

We can learn to fly beyond our dualistic condition until we are ready to leave it forever. We can begin with awareness of body, speech and mind. Understanding our true nature means understanding our relative condition and being able to reunite with its fundamental nature, so as to become like a mirror which reflects any object, manifesting its own clarity.
