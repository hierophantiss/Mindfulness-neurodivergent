The Four Forces of the Cosmos & the Fourfold Axis

An Ancient Convergence, a Modern Correspondence

About 2,500 years ago, in different corners of the world that had no communication with one another, thinkers came to the exact same realization: the world is constituted by four — or five — fundamental elements.

Empedocles in Sicily spoke of earth, water, fire, and air. The Vedic sages in India recognized the same as prithvi, jala, agni, and vayu — adding the fifth, akasha: space. In Tibet, the Bön tradition spoke of sa, chu, me, lung, and namkha. In China, the Wu Xing — with a slight variation — wood, fire, earth, metal, and water. We find this in Sufism, the Druids, Native Americans, every ancient medicine, and every mystical school.

Wherever someone observed the world, the same elements appeared — not as theoretical constructs, but as the recognition of an already existing structure. It is not a coincidence.

The Phenomenology of Presence

The elements do not describe physical matter. This is not a prehistoric periodic table. They are something far more fundamental: the ways a human mind-body naturally organizes the experience of the world.

🧱 Earth — what is stable, heavy, and resists movement. The body knows this quality. Gravity is its immediate confirmation.

💧 Water — what flows, takes shape, accommodates the other, dissolves. It is the way consciousness holds everything without becoming anything.

🔥 Fire — what alters, burns, illuminates, focuses. It is the way attention transforms whatever it touches.

💨 Air — what moves invisibly, vitalizes, touches without being caught. It is breath, the vital energy bridging body and spirit.

🌌 Space — what contains without being contained. Awareness itself, which holds all of the above without being affected.

These five phenomenological categories are almost inevitable for any conscious being. They form a basic cognitive taxonomy of experience. That is why they converge globally.

Two Lines — East and West

Two parallel paths worth examining.

The Western Line begins with Empedocles and continues to Aristotle, who adds ether — the fifth element that fills the heavens. From there, the elements pass to the Stoics, Plotinus, Hermes Trismegistus ('as above, so below'), Paracelsus, Renaissance alchemists, and finally Carl Jung in the 20th century — who recognized the four elements as the four psychic functions: sensation (earth), feeling (water), thinking (air), and intuition (fire).

The Eastern Line starts from the Upanishads (circa 800 BC), passes to Buddhist Abhidharma and the pancha mahabhuta, Tibetan medicine, and the Tantras — where elements become fields of meditative practice rather than theory. In Dzogchen, our relationship with each element is a natural state of mind, and its recognition is part of the path to Rigpa.

In Sufism, breath is the carrier of the five elements. In China, via Taoism, they become principles of movement (Tai Chi) and balance (Feng Shui). None of these traditions 'borrowed' from the other; they all started from the same direct observation of reality.

Modern Physics and the Four Forces

From the other side of human thought, modern physics arrived at an astonishingly parallel discovery: the entire universe operates through four fundamental forces.

🌌 Gravity — by far the weakest, but with infinite range, always attractive. It is what keeps us on the planet and gives weight to matter.

⚡ Electromagnetism — billions of billions of times stronger than gravity, of infinite range, dual in nature (attraction & repulsion). It binds matter into atoms and carries light.

☢️ The Weak Nuclear Force — short-ranged, responsible for particle transformation, radioactive decay, and deep changes in matter.

⚛️ The Strong Nuclear Force — the strongest of all, but on an unimaginably small scale, binding quarks into protons and protons into nuclei. Without it, matter would simply not exist.

Founding physicists like Heisenberg, Schrödinger, Bohr, Pauli, and Bohm recognized this profound relationship. It was not a metaphorical search, but the realization that the same structure reappears everywhere.

The Correspondence

Standing before these two sets, a structural symmetry begins to emerge:

🧘 Body — Earth — Gravity Gravity is the weakest force, yet we feel it every moment. Like the body: weak before the powers of the mind, yet an indescribable presence. Grounding works wherever there is weight.

💨 Breath — Air — Electromagnetism Life is an electromagnetic phenomenon. Ionic exchanges in lungs, neural conduction, the measurable EM field of the heart — all of these are electromagnetism. Breath is the bi-directional gateway (inhale & exhale, attraction & repulsion).

🎯 Attention — Fire — Weak Force The weak nuclear force transforms; it changes particles into other particles. Attention has exactly this nature: what it observes, it alters (observer effect / attention-driven cortical reorganization). Where attention goes, energy flows.

🌌 Awareness — Space — Strong Force The strong nuclear force is the strongest force in the universe, keeping matter unified. Awareness is exactly this for experience: the field that coheres and holds all the contents of experience together.

Symmetry at the Extremes

Notice what happens at the two extremes of the spectrum:

On one end, the weakest force (gravity) corresponds to the most external aspect of our experience (the body). On the other end, the strongest force (strong nuclear) corresponds to the most internal, most fundamental aspect of our experience (awareness).

'As above, so below. As without, so within.'

This maxim echo throughout mystical traditions (Emerald Tablet, Enneads, Upanishadic 'Brahman = Atman', Zhuangzi). It is not a poetic concept — it is a description of a structural reality that physics is only now beginning to recognize.

What This Means for Practice

When you ground yourself in the body, you are not metaphorically connecting with the earth. You are standing on a field governed by gravity — and at the same time training the most diffuse part of your mind to become stable.

When you breathe consciously, you stimulate the heart's electromagnetic field, regulate the autonomic nervous system, and tune into the very field through which your body communicates.

When you focus attention, you activate a network that transforms the field of experience in the exact same way the weak force transforms particles.

When you open awareness, you touch the very field that holds all the contents of your experience together — just as the strong force holds matter unified.

This method works not because of 'ancient wisdom' itself, but because it corresponds to the actual structures of the cosmos and the self.

Epilogue

About 2,500 years ago, someone stood next to a fire and said: 'This transforms.' About 100 years ago, someone solved equations and said: 'This is the weak nuclear force.'

They were not speaking of different things — they were speaking from the same field: the field of human observation probing the structure of reality.

When you practice the four-axis method, you are not alone. You stand in a 2,500-year-old lineage of thinkers, mystics, ascetics, and scientists — who all, in their own way, recognized the exact same fundamental structures.

You do not need to believe in anything. You only need to feel.
